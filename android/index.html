QuitsUp for Android Privacy Policy

Last updated 18 November 2026

Your data stays on your phone

QuitsUp keeps your Groups, people, expenses, receipts, settlements, loans, budgets and personal money on your phone. There is no QuitsUp account and no sign-in. Only a Group that you choose to share leaves your phone, encrypted, as described under Shared Groups. A Group you do not share never leaves your phone, and the developer cannot see it.

Shared Groups

If you share a Group with other people (on Android or on iPhone), its records are encrypted on your phone and stored, still encrypted, on a sharing server that the developer of QuitsUp runs on Cloudflare (Cloudflare Workers and R2 storage). The people you share the Group with can see everything saved in it: expenses, receipt photos, a place recorded on an expense, a payment line you chose to show, and a Budget the Owner sets. Editors can change it. The keys are kept on the phones of the members.

The server sees only what it needs to pass the encrypted records on: an anonymous list of the Group's devices with their public keys and roles, which phone connects and when, the network address (IP address) of the connection, the number and size of the stored records, and, for push wake-ups, the push identifier described below. There is no account: each phone proves itself with its own keys.

A receipt photo is deleted from the server once every phone in the Group has it, and after 7 days at the latest. The encrypted records of a Group stay on the server while the Group is shared and are removed after 90 days without use. Stopping sharing a Group, or deleting it, removes its encrypted records from the server. Each phone keeps its own copy, so you can keep using QuitsUp offline.

The sharing server is hosted by Cloudflare, which, as the hosting provider, processes each request and keeps standard request logs (such as the IP address, the time and the size of a request) for a limited time under its own policy. When you share a result, a statement, a reminder or a backup file yourself, Android's share sheet hands it to the app you pick, and that app's privacy policy applies.

Joining a Group nearby

To add a person who is next to you, the two phones can find each other over your local network (Wi-Fi or a hotspot) and hand over the invitation directly between the two phones. Only what the join needs goes over the local network; the Group's records themselves go through the sharing server as described above. You can also join with a pasted invitation or by scanning an invitation QR code with the camera.

What leaves your phone

QuitsUp has no analytics and no advertising. What it sends off your phone is only the following:

  • the encrypted records of a Group you share, and the details described under Shared Groups, to the sharing server;
  • when push wake-ups are on, a push identifier (see Notifications);
  • a purchase request to Google Play, if you buy something (see Purchases);
  • the exchange-rate request described next;
  • anonymous diagnostics sent by Google's own libraries (see Camera, receipts and photos).

QuitsUp itself makes one request that is not about a shared Group, and only when you open the Exchange Rate sheet for an expense or loan in a currency other than the Group's settlement currency: it asks the public exchange-rate service Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev, European Central Bank reference rates) for a suggested rate. The request contains two currency codes and a date. It contains no amounts, names, Group or expense identifiers or account. Because it is an internet request, the exchange-rate service and the networks in between can see your phone's IP address and the time of the request. QuitsUp does not do this in the background or when the app starts. A suggestion changes nothing until you tap Apply and Save, and you can type a rate without a connection. A rate fetched earlier for the same currencies and date is kept on your phone (at most 200) and still shown offline.

Location

The first time you open QuitsUp, it shows a short explanation of what notifications and location are used for, and then asks Android for permission, once. If you allow location, QuitsUp also turns on location tagging. You can turn tagging off at any time with Use my location in QuitsUp Settings, and you can change the permission in Android Settings. If you had already turned it off, QuitsUp does not turn it back on.

When location is on, QuitsUp reads your approximate location only while the app is open, one reading at a time, and never in the background. It uses it to suggest the currency of the country you are in and to tag an expense, loan or repayment with where it happened. Tagging never changes a balance, and QuitsUp works without it.

The coordinates and the time of the reading are saved in that record on your phone and are not sent to the developer by QuitsUp. If you share the Group, the place saved on an expense is part of the Group's records and is visible to its members, encrypted on the way as described under Shared Groups. Location is optional and stays off unless you turn it on. To read the location, QuitsUp uses Google Play services on your phone. To find the country, QuitsUp uses Android's built-in geocoder, which on most phones is provided by Google and may receive the coordinates; the result is a country code and nothing else is kept from that lookup. Google's own privacy policy applies to those services.

Spending Map

The Spending Map is drawn on your phone from country outlines and a city-name list that are part of the app (public-domain Natural Earth data). It shows only the places already saved with your expenses. No map service or map tiles are used and nothing is sent.

Notifications

Reminders and alerts, such as balance reminders, an evening summary, frozen-settlement reminders and budget notices, are created on your phone. QuitsUp also listens for the phone finishing a restart, only to plan its reminders again. You can turn them off in Android Settings and in QuitsUp Settings.

If you share Groups, QuitsUp can tell you when another member changes one. This notice is created on your phone after it checks the sharing server, and it holds no Group content. The setting Shared Group changes in QuitsUp Settings turns it off.

So that your phone can be told quickly while the app is closed, and when Google Play services is on your phone, QuitsUp uses Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service. While Shared Group changes is on, your phone's push identifier (a code that Google's Firebase service makes for the app on your phone) is stored on the sharing server for each Group you share, so the server can send a wake-up when someone changes the Group. The wake-up contains no Group content: it only makes your phone check the server, as it does when you open the app. The server therefore learns which push identifier belongs to which anonymous member of a Group, and the same identifier in two Groups shows that both memberships are on one device; Google learns that your phone received a wake-up at a given time. The identifier is removed from the server when you turn the setting off, leave a Group or stop sharing it (this can fail if the phone is offline), after 30 days without renewal, and when Google reports it as no longer valid. QuitsUp renews it only when it changes, when you share or join a Group, when you turn the setting on, and about once a week. A wake-up can arrive late or not at all (for example when the system restricts the app), and QuitsUp still checks the server regularly.

Your money (Personal Money)

Income, cash exchanges and exchange rates you record in Personal Money stay on your phone. They are included in your backups, are never added to a Group or shared with anyone, and are not sent to the developer. Money Flow, which shows these figures month by month, is worked out on your phone from the same records. If you turn on the Personal Money lock, Android checks your fingerprint, face or screen lock; QuitsUp never receives your biometric data.

Payment details

Payment details you add in Settings stay on your phone and are added to reminders you send. They are never sent to the developer.

Budgets

A Budget you set for a Group stays on your phone, is included in your backups, and its notifications are local to your phone. In a shared Group the Owner's Budget is part of the Group's records and visible to its members, as described under Shared Groups.

Purchases

QuitsUp is free to use with limits, and you can buy QuitsUp Plus (yearly or once), a Trip Pass for one Group, or QuitsUp Duo (yearly) in the app. Purchases are made through Google Play with your Google account. Google processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. QuitsUp never sees or stores your card or payment details, and the developer receives no personal data from a purchase.

QuitsUp asks Google Play which purchases your account holds and keeps the answer on your phone (for up to 7 days without a new answer) so that what you bought keeps working offline. When you buy a Trip Pass, QuitsUp gives Google Play the random identifier of that Group, which contains no name, so the pass can be matched to the Group; nothing else about the Group is sent. Counting what the free limits allow is done on your phone.

Camera, receipts and photos

The camera is used only when you choose to scan a receipt, take a Group cover photo, or scan an invitation QR code. Photos from your gallery use the Android photo picker, which gives QuitsUp only the photo you pick. The text on a receipt is read on your phone with Google's ML Kit text recognition, whose recognition model is part of the app. Receipt images and the text read from them are not uploaded to the developer or to any other service by the recognition. A receipt photo you attach to an expense in a shared Group is encrypted and sent to the sharing server for the other members, as described under Shared Groups. Google's SDKs (ML Kit and Google Play services) collect diagnostics data: as described by Google, this is information about how the library ran, such as device model and Android version, app name and version, an installation identifier that is not meant to identify you, and timing, image size and error codes, and never your images, your text or your Groups. This data is collected for app functionality and analytics, is sent to Google over an encrypted connection, is handled by Google as a service provider for the app, and cannot be turned off in QuitsUp. QuitsUp does not receive it. See developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure. Receipt images stay in the app's private storage on your phone. Removing a receipt image keeps its text unless you delete the expense.

Contacts

When you choose a name from your contacts for a person in a Group, QuitsUp uses Android's contact picker. QuitsUp does not hold the Contacts permission and receives only the name you pick.

Backups and exports

A backup, a CSV file or a PDF statement is a file you create in QuitsUp and save or share where you choose. A backup holds all your Groups, so keep it somewhere private. QuitsUp does not back up to any cloud on its own: its data is excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup. Android may carry the app's data to a new phone when you set one up with a cable or a device-to-device transfer; that stays between your own devices.

Widgets

Home-screen widgets show a Group's balance from a small file on your phone. While the phone is locked, widgets hide amounts and names.

No tracking, no ads

QuitsUp does not track you. It has no analytics of its own, no advertising, no sale of data and no advertising identifier. The only data leaving your phone is described under Shared Groups, What leaves your phone, Notifications, Purchases, Location and Camera, receipts and photos.

Updates

QuitsUp does not check for updates itself. Google Play delivers updates, and nothing about you or your Groups is sent by QuitsUp for that.

Children

QuitsUp is not directed to children, and it does not knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your data

Delete a Group in QuitsUp to remove it and its records from your phone. To remove a shared Group's encrypted records from the sharing server, stop sharing it or delete it as the Owner; otherwise they are removed after 90 days without use. Leaving a shared Group removes your phone's push identifier for it from the server (best effort), and the server deletes it after 30 days in any case. Uninstalling the app, or Android Settings > Apps > QuitsUp > Storage > Clear data, removes all QuitsUp data from your phone. Backup files you saved elsewhere stay until you delete them. If you want something removed from the sharing server and cannot do it in the app, write to the address below.

Changes to this policy
